What it is

BlueCity has occupied Rotterdam’s former Tropicana swimming complex since 2015. Instead of demolishing the building, it became a 12,000 m² hub where circular entrepreneurs, researchers, companies, government and education work alongside one another.

Its stated aim is to accelerate the shift from a linear economy to a circular—and ultimately blue—economy in which the output of one process becomes an input for another.

How it works

  • Shared place: more than 55 entrepreneurs, makers and researchers work from the repurposed building.
  • Material loops: residual streams can become resources for other businesses; coffee grounds growing oyster mushrooms is one of its simplest examples.
  • Biolab: the site supports experimentation in biodesign and materials made with living organisms.
  • Programmes: start-up support, chain projects, hackathons and town halls move ideas toward pilots and businesses.
  • Public interface: events and visits make circular entrepreneurship visible beyond resident companies.

Community / operating model

BlueCity’s community model is entrepreneurial rather than residential. Start-ups, researchers, established businesses, public bodies and educational institutions are deliberately mixed.

Its value comes from proximity: organisations can exchange knowledge, facilities, residual materials, introductions and customers without building every capability alone. Regular public and professional events extend the network beyond tenants.

What can be copied

  • Put circular businesses close enough to trade by-products, knowledge and opportunities.
  • Reuse a large existing building as transition infrastructure instead of waiting for a purpose-built innovation district.
  • Combine workspace with programmes that help experiments become viable businesses.
  • Give emerging materials access to labs, partners and public visibility in the same ecosystem.
