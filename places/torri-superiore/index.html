Torri Superiore

An abandoned medieval stone village restored into an inhabited ecovillage, guesthouse and learning place.

What it is

Torri Superiore is a medieval stone settlement in the Ligurian hills near Ventimiglia. A community project active since 1989 restored the deteriorated village and brought permanent life back into its dense network of rooms, passages and terraces.

Today the place combines resident community, hospitality, food, courses, cultural activity and small-scale agriculture. The official site continues to host workshops and group stays, while the community cooperative manages much of the ecotourism and cultural programme.

How it works

  • Restoration: regeneration happened through inhabiting and repairing an existing settlement rather than replacing it with a new eco-development.
  • Hospitality: guest rooms, collective meals, courses and group stays provide an economic interface between the resident community and visitors.
  • Food: local and organic food, agriculture and olive cultivation connect the settlement to its surrounding landscape.
  • Learning: workshops and programmes turn community experience into something visitors can participate in rather than only observe.
  • Shared infrastructure: common dining, activity and social spaces allow the historic fabric to support contemporary collective life.

Community / operating model

The resident community is supported by a wider organisational structure rather than functioning only as a collection of private households. The Ture Nirvane community cooperative manages ecotourism, courses, seminars and cultural activity.

Current community-cooperative information describes roughly twenty resident adults and children, alongside non-resident members and guests. Visitors are folded into the place through shared meals, stays, training and volunteering rather than being separated into a conventional hotel experience.

What can be copied

  • Let hospitality finance cultural and community infrastructure without turning the whole place into a resort.
  • Restore historic fabric by giving it an active contemporary use instead of freezing it as heritage scenery.
  • Use shared meals and common spaces as operating infrastructure for community, not just amenities.
  • Treat visitors as temporary participants in a system they can understand.

Yogawiser read

Torri Superiore is a reminder that regeneration is sometimes less about inventing new architecture than finding a social organism capable of keeping old architecture alive.
