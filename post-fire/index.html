Methods, assumptions & limitations

The relative cover index is generated with a bounded exponential recovery curve, using hypothetical modifiers for fire severity, rainfall, seed availability and ecosystem type. Assisted and active pathways increase the assumed rate, while high severity can reduce active intervention success. These coefficients are deliberately illustrative, not fitted to observational data. The chart is not a prediction for any actual location.

Erosion concern is an ordinal screening heuristic based on severity, slope and rainfall. Actual post-fire risks also depend on soil, hydrophobicity, storm intensity, burn pattern, geology, catchment and downstream exposure. Site photographs here are displayed only and never analysed. Restoration decisions require local ecology, safety checks, land ownership permissions, and qualified field assessment.

Next research stage: integrate validated burn severity (e.g. Sentinel-2 dNBR), site-specific habitat layers, monitored regeneration time series, erosion models and uncertainty ranges with field verification. A high severity score alone does not imply tree planting is appropriate.
