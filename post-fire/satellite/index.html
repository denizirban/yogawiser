04 / LONGITUDINAL SATELLITE OBSERVATIONS

2021–2026 · Recovery signal

Measure the mean NBR of selected summer satellite scenes at the same coordinates over successive years. NBR can reflect vegetation changes, but is not a direct measurement of ecological recovery or a forecast.

Run the post-fire analysis above to unlock a multi-year comparison.

Each year uses the lowest overall cloud-cover candidate found between 12 August and 30 September. Comparisons may still be biased by phenology, differing scene footprints, radiometric offsets and cloud artefacts. Yearly acquisition date, valid pixel coverage and scene IDs are shown for traceability. A failed year is shown as missing, never interpolated.

Scientific method, limitations and data provenance

Scene search uses Element 84 Earth Search STAC v1, collection sentinel-2-l2a. The workbench accesses the scenes' NIR (B8, 10 m), SWIR2 (B12, 20 m), and scene classification (SCL, 20 m) Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs, resampling to a common 96×96 rectangular grid. Values are digital-number-scale reflectance proxies; normalized indices are calculated from each pair, with checks for zero, missing and invalid pixels. Sentinel-2 L2A processing baseline and offsets may change reflectance interpretation, so cross-date harmonization should be confirmed before publishing scientific results. Both-date SCL pixels classified as cloud, shadow, snow or no-data are excluded; a full two-date atmospheric and quality-screening pipeline is NOT implemented. Global scene cloud percentages do not ensure this individual site is cloud-free. Mean and median summarize this experimental sample only. For any operational decision, use validated cloud/shadow masking on both dates, radiometric harmonization, site-ground-truth calibration, spatially appropriate dNBR thresholds, local ecological expertise and uncertainty estimates. Maps are not burn perimeters or recovery forecasts.
